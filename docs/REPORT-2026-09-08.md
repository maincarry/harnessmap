# HarnessMap — measurement report, 2026-09-08

Two audiences, two registers: §A in plain words; §B as it goes in the ledger.

## A. In plain words

**What the product does.** When you work with an AI assistant for weeks, it forgets: each
session starts nearly blank, and the raw chat history is too long to hand back. HarnessMap
watches those conversations and files what was decided into a map — a tree of topics with
one current statement per topic and a history of how it changed — and hands the assistant
the part of the map that matters for what you are asking now.

**What we measured.** Can the assistant answer questions about a project's own history
better with the map than with the same amount of raw chat history? We wrote 69 questions
about this product's own six-week build (each with a known right answer), asked a sealed
assistant each question three times with the map, and three times with an equal-sized
slice of the raw record. Two independent graders scored every answer 0, 1 or 2.

**What we found (2026-09-08).**

| | With the map | With raw history |
|---|---|---|
| Right answer | 73% | 20% |
| Hedged / didn't know | 18% | 50% |
| Wrong answer | 9% | 30% |
| Average score (0–2) | 1.65 | 0.89 |

The map wins on 50 of the 69 questions, loses on 8, ties on 11. The two graders agreed on
355 of the 414 answers. Over four rounds of improving the product this week the map's
score rose from 1.20 to 1.65 while the raw-history score stayed between 0.82 and 0.90.

**What it means.** The assistant remembers what the project decided far better through the
map than through the same amount of raw history, and each product change we made moved
the number in the direction we predicted. It also builds the map correctly from live
conversation: across two replayed stretches of 25 real rounds each, every documented
decision was filed and placed in every run, and the map showed the latest ruling as
current on 4 of 5 and 5 of 6 checks; the misses are old wordings left standing on
neighbouring notes (see §B).

**What it does not mean yet.** The questions were written by the builder, about our own
project; an outside project and outside questions are untested. The map used its full
context allowance; whether it does as well with less is a separate run. "Remembers" is
not "does the work": a task-based test is next. Two of the 69 answer keys turned out to
be stale, which counts against the map, not for it.

**What is next.** A task-based test (five real deliverables, map vs raw history); a second
filing segment (running); questions written by the founders; and the preferences design.

## B. As it goes in the ledger

**Design.** Pre-registered paired comparison on a frozen bank of 69 questions about this
project's record (45 lot-drawn from the numbered entries with a fixed seed, 24
builder-chosen), three repetitions per cell, two arms answered by the same sealed agent
(no tools; canary-verified before every run): (a) the v6 map, aimed by the product before
every question — auto-focus reads the question, auto-light lights under a budget guard,
consented pull-ups of set-aside topics — briefing median 41k characters; (b) the most
recent slice of the raw record cut to the same character budget per question. Blind 0–2
grading against a required fact per item (rubric v2: central claim, then
agrees/partial/declines/contradicts; declining is never 0) by one model, re-graded in
full by a second. Cohen's kappa 0.77 (n = 414), above the .70 floor. Noise floor from
repeat variance 0.18.

**Result.** Map 1.65, transcript 0.89. Paired mean difference 0.75, bootstrap 95% CI 0.55
to 0.95; sign 50–8–11. The registered claim (difference ≥ 0.3) is supported; the entire
interval lies above the bar and above the noise floor. Under the second grader alone the
difference is 0.73. 51 of 69 questions recalled (mean ≥ 1.5); 3 at zero.

**Progression, same bank and transcript arm.**

| run | condition | map | transcript | Δ | kappa | judged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-06 08:48 | v5, aimed once | 1.20 | 1.05 | 0.14 | 0.71 (n=81) | not supported |

**Scope of the claim.** Holds for this project, this record, a map built and aimed by the
product from the same record, and questions phrased by the builder in the record's own
vocabulary (for the user asking about their own conversation this is the realistic
condition; for a second reader it is a caveat). Two items (B25, B28) carry stale required
facts and count against the map. The margin was obtained at the map's full 40k budget;
equal quality at a smaller budget is untested. The lighter was refused 13 times for
proposing over the lit cap (the guard held). Convergent evidence from the task test (Test
3) is required before a headline claim about work rather than recall. External validity
beyond this project is untested.

**Filing (Test 1).** A frozen 25-round segment of real conversation (2026-08-23,
22:18–23:40) replayed through the production filer into an empty map, three runs, graded
by frozen code checks. Ruling class (each documented commitment exists, statused,
placed): 15/15 in every run. Currency class (the later ruling reads live, the earlier
dead; a resolved question not left open): 5/5, 4/5, 4/5 — median 4/5, at the 80% floor.
The misses are real: a task node kept "toggle restored live" after the round that dropped
the toggle; an open "delete or keep?" after its answer. Second segment, the same morning
(08:07–09:41: a UI redesign rewound, zoom made view-only, a recency-rule reversal, the
tutorial rulings; 16 ruling + 6 currency checks), three runs: ruling 16/16 in every run;
currency 6/6, 5/6, 4/6 — median 5/6, at the floor. The three real misses share one shape
with the evening's: the filer rewrites the node the round is about and misses a bystander
that repeats the old rule (a rewind summary still saying "time-decay as shipped", a note
still describing the rewound warm-row encoding, a Q&A still answering "only while zoomed").
