# What the VC sees

**By 0:20.** A product for people who work with coding agents. The pain is familiar: hours of decisions inside a session, gone when it compacts or ends.

**By 1:10.** The core. An engineer talks to Claude Code as usual, and beside her a map writes itself: her decisions in her own words with her name on them, a rule that would be forgotten, a correction kept as a correction, an open question, a next step. Nobody typed into the map. This is the moment they lean in or they do not.

**By 2:00.** It carries forward. A new session days later has the map in its context and picks up the open question. A small desktop companion says the map is up and takes you back to the session.

**By 2:20.** You can ask the map and it answers from its own record. The same map can be looked at as a sky.

**By 3:00.** Why now: every engineer now works through an agent harness; the harness is where the thinking happens and nobody keeps it. We keep it, locally, with a two-line install. The memory layer for agent work.

## The question they will ask afterwards

"Why does this not get built into Claude Code or Codex?"

Our answer: it may, for one harness each. HarnessMap sits across harnesses and across sessions, on the user's machine, in the user's own files, and the map is the product, not a feature of a chat window. The harness vendors own the conversation; we own what was discussed.

## The second question

"How do I know it files the right things?"

Our answer: we replay real conversations through it every day and read every node it files. Tested against real conversations every day, no numbers on screen.
